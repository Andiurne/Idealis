Hawen couldn't remember the last time his desk had been this clean. The letter cut a staunch white picture on the mahogany, and the inky black of his lettering caught the last rays of sunset through the window. His garnet pen rested neatly to the side, just beneath an ornate ink well finally run dry. His lecture notes sat neatly organized in shelves beneath the window, meticulously catalogued by subject in the hours he'd spent since sealing the parchment. They had previously found their home just above everywhere in the room besides the desk, and Hawen had no doubt they'd return within days. But she would complain, rightly, if they remained there too long.

Hawen turned to open their closet, running his fingers over the dusty cotton of her sweaters before settling on the rough wool of his greatcoat and tossing it over his shoulders. In a swift, choreographed motion, he swayed across the room back to his desk and slid the letter into his breast pocket, nearly stepping out the door before he caught himself at the threshold. He almost forgot.

"Until the evening, love."

Gyllicadajë Monefeorforma, Autumn 1009

To my dear Roädea,

It’s been some weeks since I last wrote to you,

and for that I sincerely apologize.

Preparing Hütha for university has taken up

no small amount of my time. Your colleagues in

the arterial college are excited to welcome her;

I believe they have every faith she’ll follow in your

footsteps. I myself am inclined to believe them.

She still holds that same passion that first

drew me to you, not only for the workings of the body,

but for helping the souls that inhabit them.

She’ll make a fine doctor.

The soft crunch of leaves on pavement accompanied the clack of Hawen's heels as he made his way up the stone-paved streets of Haavenküth, raising a hand to shield his eyes as he savored the occasional glimpse of gilded amber light reflecting off the westward bay betwixt brick-and-mortar townhouses as the Sun slowly left his sight. It would be gone in a few short hours. Hardly any time at all.

The ink you gave me before departing runsdry as I write this. Though I'm sure the penwill occupy my desk a while longer; your judgmentserved you well in commissioning it from Alferhthiuer.The flashing streaks of red remind me of your eyes,and help me to keep their image in my mind asyour journey lengthens. Hütha holds the scarf youleft for her in similar regard; I expect she'llbe wearing it during the inauguration, despitethe dean's best attempt at "preserving the sanctityof uniform." I'll find a way to convince them.

As the buildings reached lower and grew sparse along the sidewalk, Hawen found himself leaving the city for the hilltop forests dyed in brown and gold. The skyline lay entirely beneath him, attached firmly to the ground as he seemed to rise above the evening fog. Terracotta rooftops painted the hillside, divided by the lilac of everviolet trees and the banners modeled after their color, flown in recognition of the autumnal equinox: Remembrance Day.

I swear, as time goes by, I see more of youin her. It shouldn't scare me as much as it does.I know you. I know you're doing good, no matter whereyou might be right now. It never mattered who it was,nor when, nor where. You could never stand to see someonesuffer, for even a single second. Hütha carries thatspark in her too. She claims it what draws her to thefield, and I don't think it's a lie.But I do know part of her chases it becauseit brings her closer to you, in a way.But that's just a father's intuition.

As he crested the top of the hill, the full majesty of Witafjürgen University came into view. The hues of sunset painted its marble walls like a brush upon canvas, while its observatory spires reached upward to scrape the clouds as the hand of a child would reach off the side of a canoe to briefly touch the ocean's awesome splendor. It was that natural awe for the world which had inhabited Roädea and him, and drew them together in their studies here. The same wonder that inhabited their daughter. The same wonder that led him to teach here, hoping to cultivate it in the next round of aspirant firekeepers, before it was inevitably snuffed out by the assurance of an old man that he'd seen everything worth seeing. But for today, at least, his attention was drawn to a well-maintained garden just outside the walls.

When Hütha first learned of these letters,I made a promise to myself and to herthat the ink well would determine their end.I thought better of refilling it after the first month.So this shall be the final time I write to you.

Even in fall, some flowers still bloomed. Hawen admired their strength in the face of the inevitable as he stood before the stone.

Were you here, I know you would insist I hopeit be a long while before we meet again.Though a part of me wishes otherwise.Selfish, I know. It's become quieter lately.
I'm here because it's what you would want.I'm here for Hütha.In time, I hope, I'll be here for myself too.

Your love eternal,

Hawen

Setting it gently beneath the headstone, Hawen gazed at the mixture of finality and memory for a brief moment. Then, with his mind made up, Hawen set the envelope alight in offering with a compressed bolt of plasma from his gloved hand, before setting back off towards town to meet Hütha for supper. The inscription, kept clean through the seasons, read succinctly:

Roädea Liffhälend-Sijëlwürta

Autumn 983 - Spring 1009

Beloved Mother

Respected Scholar

Valiant Medic
