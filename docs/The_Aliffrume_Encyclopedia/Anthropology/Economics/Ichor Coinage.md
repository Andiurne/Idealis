# Ichor Coinage
Firekeeper-produced ichor coinage is a near-ubiquitous type of physical currency.

The phlogistic signature of ichor is unalterable once produced, and is easily distinguishable from others by a trained appraiser. Moreover, reproducing coinage with the signature of another is currently thought impossible. This does present the problem of needing a trusted signatory to standardize the currency, who will eventually die or otherwise need to be replaced, but so long as proper care is taken to encourage recycling, this is an inconvenience at best every 30 years or so.

Ichor coins are manufactured to a standard size with varying shapes, in such a way that larger denominations cannot be produced by subtracting from smaller ones. This is sufficient to limit forgery to generation, as while coins are easily carved and sanded, no methods of melding ichor exist.

Often multiple signatories will assist in coin production, which further compounds the difficulty of reproductive forgeries. This also limits the exploitability of the process, eases transitions, and helps with volume demands for the high turnover inherent to the process. Some larger countries will even allow for currency which has a subset of the current signatories, expanding the amount of time currency may be used for before recycling.

The final signature on coinage is referred to as the gestalt, and financial institutions are issued identity samples of currently accepted gestalts (often the current one, and one or two past ones, depending on how much currency is in circulation in the town.) 

## In Gimyndine Cultures
In gimyndine-based societies, the process is much simpler, as the sign of coinage can be tied to a physical, specific sample. However, weaker gimyndine samples have a much weaker presence, and also can fail to meet generation requirements, so often a mint's signatory stone will be notably strong.
