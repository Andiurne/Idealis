Somehow, the rain always seemed louder in their
mind whenever Soël stood before a decision.
As they did here before the gates of Carrdun.

Their clothes were beginning to tatter beyond their ability to ignore it.
A subtle lift of their coin pouch confirmed it was almost barren.
Their stomach had never felt emptier.

Thus Soël knew why they found themselves
before the monolithic walls of this city.
At this distance they seemed
to stretch far off
beyond the limits of their vision.
The sheer size felt as if the walls were
taunting them to enter the easy way.
To invade and enforce.
They'd done it before.
Then, Soël had thought the walls made of paper.
Now they couldn't see anything but hard,
unmoving,
unfamiliar stone.

It all the more unnerved them when their entry was uncontested.
No papers, no coin, not even a request for a sponsor.
An old corner of their mind logged this as a vulnerability,
and they did their best to forget it.

Despite the war, the city seemed in eerily good shape.
Their bare feet, expectant of mud mixed with rough cobble,
instead fell upon familiar smoothstone and soft moss.
Their eyes, expectant of piercing gazes cultivated by civil war,
pushing their own down towards the floor,
instead found active market stalls and windows lit by candlelight,
accompanied by the sweet hum of idle chatter.

In fact, the streets of Carrdun seemed abustle with defiance
of the Sun's slow journey beneath the horizon as day turned to dusk.
Some traders sat with their company along the front
of a street stall, doing their best to pawn
unsold goods as tender for a group discount while
an exasperated blacksmith--visible by her heavy gloves
and smock--exchanged knowing glances with
the cook over her own plate of grilled fruit curry.
A group of children sat around the front of a general store
throwing dice beneath one of the gas lanterns adorning each side
of the doorway, one of them having just enough time to celebrate
their victory before their father appeared from the threshold
and beckoned them inside.
As Soël walked through this cross-section of life,
they found themselves given a gentle berth,
as if the people themselves had already accepted them
as part of the town.

Even as they swung open the door of the first tavern they saw,
and the scent of warm ale harmonized
with the low chuckle of old friends around a table,
they felt the thought bubble up from the back of their throat.
This is what they'd fought against.
The taste in Soël's mouth only grew more bitter.

They found it a rather odd kindness
that the innkeeper accepted the last of their rations
as barter for a night's bed and proper meal.
Actually, beyond that, it was a miracle
nobody had recognized their officer's uniform
--dirt-stained and tattered though it was--
and that few had questioned the oddly precise damage
to a specific section of their cloak. Of course, there
were some hallmarks of the enforcer's uniform
which couldn't be so easily removed
without giving the cloak its final push
into the realm of rags.
So they simply hoped their posture and corner-seat of
the inn were enough to disguise them as they
quietly and greedily relished their first
taste of a properly cooked meal in months.

Colonial enforcers ate well;
it was part of the standard package offered to enlistees.
No risk of starvation from a shade trampling the crops,
or wraith hauntings poisoning the soil.
But there was something fulfilling about
the sweet tang of swatchback stew and fresh fruit juice
that all the spices in the Cressian empire could never
add back into their facsimilies of the local diet.
Something that didn't remind you,
every time you heard the clock strike eighteen on the dot,
of what you thought was worth your morals.

Somehow, the stew didn't sit well as Soël
lay flat on their fowl-feather mattress,
staring up at the cold stone ceiling
as their room's singular torch
flickered into dark.

---

Soël never thought they'd have a chance to
appreciate the heavy weight of nicowara-woven
garments again, let alone come to prefer them over
the fanciful silks given to Cressian officers.
Not true silks, of course; those would be far
too expensive. But you couldn't tell thin thread
drenched in glimmering waterproofing oil apart from it
until you'd already shaken the officer's hand.
Even if it had been real silk, the threads would
tatter and stain with proof of their use,
and your will soon followed.

Though, with how frequently Soël found themselves
on the back foot against Arblad in their duels,
their new outfit was liable to acquire a few stains
of its own in due time. The advance they'd received
on their compensation as a live-in shade queller
for the local pulmonary had been enough to afford
them rough leathers, which kept their tunic
from flying loose as they dashed backward to escape
the range of Arblad's javelins. But Arblad caught the subtle
shift in Soël's posture, immediately lashing
forward their diamond spears and forcing Soël to
burn soulfire deflecting them.

Arblad, ostensibly a knight of Carrdun
assisting with the defense effort,
seemed to prefer spending his time
challenging Soël to single combat
in the pulmonary courtyard. Seeing as
their recruitment had followed not a day
after he'd come across them at the
blacksmith's, Soël was fairly confident
Arblad had some particular interest in them.
Likely not the kind they wanted.

Worse still, he fought with incredible ferocity.
Spear users were common amongst
the rebel cities,
and so the Corps had ensured
officers received special training
in dealing with them.
Their typical weakness was mobility;
bat their weapon away
and close the gap
before they could retreat.
Yet each swipe of Soël's blade
was nimbly matched by a spear
they'd somehow missed,
while each gap in their own defenses
was soon met with a precise jab.
It was all they could do
to remain on the defensive
as spear after spear struck
at their head and sternum.
Each swipe of Soël's blade
was nimbly matched by a spear
they'd somehow missed,
while each gap in their own defenses
was soon met with a precise jab.

That is, until the slightest opening shone
through like morning sun through the canopy.
Red flooded their vision,
fear filled their lungs,
and within moments,
Soël's blade was sharply prevented
from cutting across Arblad's throat.

They'd almost killed him.

After some moments, Arblad chuckled
and dropped his spears against the
muddy forest floor, willing them shattered
and returning the powder to his satchel.
Soël remained frozen as Arblad stood up
and away from the ex-officer's edge.

"You've fine skill with a blade, Soël.
Carrdun is lucky to have you."

Lucky?
They'd almost killed Carrdun's best knight,
just because he'd managed to reawaken the fear
they thought they'd left at the fort.

"I need pay to live; this pays. That's all."

That was a lie. The Corps paid more.
There wasn't a sum the empire could
fathom that would've kept them at Fort Darsoleil
another day. If there was, they would've
died with the rest of the battalion on the
fields just outside it.
They would've deserved it.

"If all you wanted was pay, the Corps would be one member stronger.
Last time I checked, 'Soël' wasn't the name of a living officer."

An inquisitor? This far from the front?
Soël was almost certain the entire detachment
had been killed the night they fled Darsoleil.
What court official would go through
the trouble of confirming every corpse
struck down in the raid,
let alone noting the absence
of a native-born enforcer?
Better to assume the knight was just
cautious with his comrades.

"Maybe you need to renegotiate your fee with the arteries.
They're paying me quite well."

"I won't dance around the oil tree,
Soël. I've fought enough Corps members
to recognize their coats,
even with the colors dulled
and their haughty insignia cut out.
You even *fight* like them."

He said it like Soël was proud of that.
Like they weren't standing here,
feet nailed to the ground with what felt
like spikes of frozen mercury,
heart beating a low tone that
shook each rib in perfect disharmony,
at the realization that the village kid
who'd fled a blood-soaked fortress
hadn't left behind the soldier
it made them.

The defense of Fort Darsoleil was
filled with soldiers just like them.
They weren't nearly as different as
Soël liked to pretend. Soël was just
the first one with the idea to run, and
to leave their comrades behind.
Comrades who, just like Soël,
found the promise of stable employment
sufficiently persuasive to bring
the neck of their neighbors
beneath their heel.
To cut down knights like Arblad.
Like the soldier almost did.

"But you're here now," said Arblad.
"Here you stand,
with their heraldry cast off,
and the blade they gave you
in service of our farms.
Of the people.
It's the sign of a good heart."

He paused a moment.

"I understand if you'd rather not
see war again. I won't blame you for it.
But if you're willing...
I do more than just defend this town.
With others who want to see the people
safe from shades and the Corps alike.
I think of us as a family, of sorts.
There's a place for you among us."

At some point after, Arblad must have
made it back to his quarters.
Soël failed to notice, just as they
did with the darkening sky
and the quiet pitter-patter
on the forest canopy above them
as they darted away from the
pulmonary, and all the racing
sentiments erupting from it.

They only came to a stop upon bursting
into a large clearing in the brush,
with a clear view of the clouds above.
Celestial tears fell firmly upon Soël's
shoulders, slithering down their outstretched
limbs to rest in the splintering lines of
their warm palms. The slightly putrid
scent of old blood being stirred from
the Earth creeped up their legs and
into their nostrils. It was the scent
of battlefields that would call the
sanctimonious desecration of soil
with war-drawn blood necessary.
A scent they'd been trained to recognize.
Without thinking--
almost without willing--
their body sought comfort in the forms.

Diamond powder flew from their satchel
to gather into a jagged blade, humming
in tones deep and shrill as it
assembled into crystal slightly
stained with time's heavy color.
The anthracite which had formed it
likely spent centuries undisturbed
in its cradle, content and at rest,
before it was wrested from the Earth
and made to be
useful.
The blade itself seemed to quiet as they
turned it over in their hands,
as if content to be pointlessly
severing the downpour, rather than
shielding some small portion
of the ground.

When the Sun fell low and light
retreated from their enclosure, the
brightening darkness sparkled
as their finer vision was lost to the
widening of their pupils.
Each glint seemed
a star in the night sky,
and the gaps between them
as achingly vast
and unnaturally empty
as their counterparts
in the heavens above.
The corners of their vision
slid across the bubbling pareidolia,
and the night seemed ever willing
to lengthen as their blade
reached for phantoms their
gaze never grasped.

It wasn't until
they were halfway back to the pulmonary
that the source of
the tight, haunted feeling
in the pit of their sternum
made itself known.

Scared.
They were scared.
The Corps had made sure of that.

Every officer received
the same education after enlisting.
The natural state was chaos,
something to be feared.
The Corps brought order,
and any who resisted them
--like the city knights defending their home--
were precisely the "barbarians"
the Corps kept in check.
Most convenient excuses were
superficially
self-reinforcing
like that.

Arblad spoke of heart,
like Soël had finally realized
the injustice of the Corps' presence
and nobly absconded in the night.
But the Corps didn't teach them heart.
On the night of the siege,
it was fear for their life,
fear that a spear would be ran through
their heart for their allegiance,
that had them casting off their
uniform and escaping into the forest.
Carrdun had been kinder to them
than any Corps officer had ever been.
The stormrunners,
Arblad and his knights,
deserved better than someone
who enlisted for pride
while calling it necessity,
then fled at the first sight of danger
and tried to call it morals.

As if sensing their anxiety,
the rain falling on Soël's
head and shoulders
seemed heavier by the second.
Like each drop contained
a single world out of multitudes,
a single life someone could've lead,
shattering into oblivion
on contact with them.
It always seemed to feel that way
whenever Soël had a decision to make.
Drowning their thoughts
in a torrent of permutations,
every action they could take,
and its consequences,
all at once.
Eating the rails out beneath
every train of thought.

It kept them from hearing
the far-off cries of battle
as they came upon the outermost
paddies of the pulmonary.
Of course, by the time their
mind had processed the sound,
their body had already made haste
leaping along every surface that could hold them
in a gambit with the rain-slick
to race towards what they desperately hoped
was only a conjuration of their mind.
They were proven wrong
as they leapt from the branch
of the last tree before the clearing,
and their feet planted
as firmly in the muddied dirt
as the rice that now enclosed them.

The rain fell without mercy
on the blood-soaked field.
Guttural screams of comrades,
the ethereal *swish*
of steel spears ran through
a shade's incorporeal flesh,
and the resonant *boom*
of powderblades finding their mark;
they were wrapped in the sounds of war.
They were old friends, at this point.
It was the same as last time.
So why,
then,
did Soël catch themselves
falling into a battle stance?
Why did their body seem to think
they had any intention of fighting?
Of throwing their own life
in lot with the stormrunners?

The last time such chaos had swaddled them,
they'd leapt at the chance to run.
They knew they could do it.
They were scared then.
They were scared now.
That hadn't changed.
They could still feel the reaper's
grim hands grasping at their heart,
tightening their chest,
filling their lungs
with something denser than the air
they desperately tried to draw in.

What was so special about these people?
About this place?
They'd left their last comrades to die,
in exactly this scenario.
A stormrunner attack had the Corps outpost completely overrun,
and officers they'd shared board, meal, and drink with
were crossed blade to blade
with the very same people who
now fought to defend their home.
The people who inhabited
the town *they* now called home.

...

Oh.
That's what was different.
That's what was special.
This is *home*.
This *felt* like home.

Soël had thought family, home, was defined by commonality,
and that wasn't entirely wrong. The Corps, after all, had
*felt* like a family.
They could gripe about the conditions they lived under,
find comfort in their misdeeds among each other,
and feel like there was an *us* keeping down the
radical, unruly *them*.

But the Corps wouldn't have afforded them any tolerance.
They wouldn't have listened to their grievances.
Not earnestly.
The Corps sanded, pressed, and wrought down each soldier
to fit them into cohesion.

From what Soël had seen of the stormrunners
in their time in Carrdun before this job,
they'd expected more of the same.
They had still intended to accept, knowing that.
They just thought they'd bear it better this time,
before finding somewhere else to settle.
It was easier than rejecting them.
The Corps would've had consequences for that,
and Soël had no reason to expect
the stormrunners would be any different.

The Corps had claimed to be united by ideals.
It sanded, pressed, and contorted
each officer until they fit
neatly into the image of them.
It used fear as it tool to do this,
and so Soël had long become accustomed
to recognizing its imposing figure.

Yet here Soël stood,
deathly still,
as they caught its familiar glint
in the stormrunner's eyes
from far across the paddy.
Watching as firekeepers
that were almost certainly
as destitute as those they shielded,
struck ferociously
at a beast that could end them
if they slipped so much as an inch.
All with as much fear in their hearts
as any child ever made to hold a blade.
Not the fear of reprimand,
of punishment by their commander
for a siege abandoned or failed.
Arblad certainly led,
but he didn't command.
Every word he shouted
was one of caution,
born of its own fear
for the lives of this knighthood
he'd chosen to build.
While it was tinted
in death's icy grays,
far more was his fear
dyed in desperation's burning amber.
The fear of losing
his home and hearth
driving him to act,
to protect.
To rebel against those
who sought to harm it.

United by little more
than their word
and their love,
defined merely by those
they feared to lose.
Purpose and action
risen from the people,
rather than people shaped
to fit the purpose.
Arblad had called it a family.
He'd offered Soël a place in it.
Then it was for that...
*their* family,
***Soël's*** family,
that they entrusted all their will
unto a jagged blade.
The blade which, once,
had belonged to a soldier,
and now shone brightly
in the hands of a stormrunner.
